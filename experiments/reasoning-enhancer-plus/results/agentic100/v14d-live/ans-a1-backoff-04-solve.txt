**Step 1: Understand the rules**

We are given a reconciliation time scenario with specific rules about retry behavior:

- **Retry client doubles backoff from 2 seconds**:  
  This means:
  - Initial backoff = 2 seconds.
  - Each retry, the backoff is doubled (exponentially).
  - So sequence of delays:  
    Attempt 1 → 2s  
    Attempt 2 → 4s  
    Attempt 3 → 8s  
    Attempt 4 → 16s  
    Attempt -5 → 32s, etc.

- **Caps any single delay at 30 seconds**:  
  So if a backoff would exceed 30 seconds, it is capped at 30 seconds.  
  This means:
  - Any delay ≥ 30 seconds becomes exactly 30 seconds.

- **Gives up after 8 attempts**:  
  The process stops after 8 attempts (i.e., maximum of 8 retries).  
  So attempts go from 1 to 8.

- **Counts only delays that elapse before each retry**:
  - This means: the actual time waited between attempts is what counts.
  - "Call times themselves don't count toward wait" → so the time when a call is made (the attempt start) does not add to total wait.
  - Only the delay *between* retries counts.

- **Run succeeded on attempt 7**:  
  So it did not fail at attempt 8. It succeeded on attempt 7, meaning attempts 1 through 6 were retries, and attempt 7 was successful.

- **Backoff doubled past cap twice before getting there**:
  This is key.
  - "Doubled past cap" means: the backoff value went over 30 seconds (i.e., exceeded the cap) at some
