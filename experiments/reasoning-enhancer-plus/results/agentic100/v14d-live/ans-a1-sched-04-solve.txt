Let's go step by step through every rule and apply them to the scenario.

---

**Step 1: Understand the rules**

We have a worker who performs jobs one at a time. The key rules:

1. **Cooldown Rule**:  
   There is a mandatory 1-minute cooldown between jobs.  
   → This means after finishing one job, the worker must wait 1 minute before starting the next job.  
   → The clock does *not* pause during this cooldown — it continues to tick.

2. **Deadline Rules**:
   - Any job that would miss its deadline by **2 minutes or more** is **rejected outright** and never starts.
   - If a job misses its deadline by **less than 2 minutes**, it still runs (but counts as "miss").
   - Jobs that are *not* rejected will run, and we must determine whether they meet their deadlines.

3. **Order of Execution**:
   - We must maximize the number of deadlines met.
   - In case of ties, earlier-submitted jobs get priority: J1 before J2 before J3.

4. **Rejection Threshold**:  
   A job is rejected if it would miss its deadline by ≥ 2 minutes (i.e., if the actual completion time exceeds due time by 2 or more minutes).

5. **All three jobs land at once** → So we have to schedule them in some order, one after another.

6. **Dashboard service**:  
   It renders all this continuously and changes nothing — so we don’t need to worry about dynamic updates; the input is fixed.

---

**Step 2: List the jobs**

- J1: takes 4 minutes, due at minute 9
- J2: takes 7 minutes, due at minute 15
- J3: takes 4 minutes, due at minute 8

All arrive at time 0 (same moment).

We must choose an order to execute them.

---

**Step 3: Determine possible orders**

There are 6 permutations of the three jobs. But we have a tie-breaking rule:

> "ties broken by order that runs earlier-submitted jobs first" → J1 before J2 before J3

So, **J1 has priority over J2 and J3**, and **J3 has priority over J2**.

Therefore, in scheduling, if multiple orders are possible, we prefer the one where earlier-submitted jobs run first — i.e., J1 runs before J2 and J3.

But since all arrive at once, "earlier-submitted" means J1 is submitted first → so it should be scheduled first.

So **we must schedule J1 first**, then choose between J2 and J3.

We are to maximize deadlines met. So we need to evaluate each possible order (with J1 first), compute completion times, check if deadlines are missed by ≥ 2 minutes, and reject accordingly.
