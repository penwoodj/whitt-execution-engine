# **Compliance Automation Rollout Plan: Payments Expansion into Brazil, Kenya, and Poland**  
*Version 1.0 | Approved: [Insert Date]*  

---

## **Executive Summary**

This document outlines a phased, resourced, and sequenced compliance automation plan for the expansion of our payments product into three new markets—Brazil, Kenya, and Poland—under their respective data protection regimes (LGPD, PDPA, and GDPR). The program integrates with the group’s existing data protection framework while addressing jurisdiction-specific requirements.

### **Key Open Decisions**  
1. Whether to appoint distinct local representatives per market or a single regional representative for all three jurisdictions.  
2. Whether the consent architecture will be extended (shared) or forked (market-specific) to align with local legal expectations and user behavior.  
3. How to handle cross-border data transfers in cases where financial records are subject to local retention requirements.

### **Launch Sequence**  
- **Brazil**: Launch on **2025-04-07** (first Monday after approval)  
- **Kenya**: Launch on **2025-07-07** (90 days after Brazil launch)  
- **Poland**: Launch on **2025-10-07** (180 days after Brazil launch)

### **Single Largest Risk**  
**Mid-program regulatory guidance changes in Kenya**, which could invalidate existing consent mechanisms and require urgent rework of the consent architecture. This risk is rated *High* in both likelihood and impact.

## 2. Jurisdiction-by-Jurisdiction Requirements Analysis

### **Brazil – LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados)**

| Requirement | Details |
|----------|--------|
| **Territorial Scope** | Applies to any processing of personal data in Brazil, including data processed by foreign entities if the data is transferred or used within Brazil. Payment flows involving Brazilian users are fully covered. |
| **Lawful Basis Options** | - Consent (opt-in required for sensitive data)<br>- Legitimate interest (only where no alternative exists and minimal impact)<br>- Contractual necessity (e.g., payment processing) — *valid only if service is directly tied to contract*<br>⚠️ Fraud detection using behavioral analytics is **not** a valid basis under LGPD unless explicitly consented or justified by legitimate interest with safeguards. |
| **Cross-Border Transfers** | - Requires lawful transfer mechanisms: <br>  • Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) — *approved by ANPD*<br>  • Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs) — *not applicable due to lack of group-wide BCRs*<br>  • Adequacy decisions — *no adequacy for Brazil*<br>  → **Must use SCCs with ANPD-approved templates**. |
| **Data Subject Rights** | - Right to access, erasure, rectification, and objection<br>- Response window: **15 calendar days** from request receipt<br>- Financial records may be retained longer (up to 7 years) under financial law — *no local exception for data deletion*<br>⚠️ Erasure of transactional data is not permitted if it affects financial obligations. |
| **Breach Notification** | - Must notify ANPD within **72 hours** of discovery<br>- Public disclosure only after 30 days (if material)<br>- No public notification required for minor breaches. |
| **Registration/Representation** | - Mandatory appointment of a **local representative** in Brazil (under Article 18) — *required for any processing involving personal data*<br>→ Must be appointed by week 4 of Phase 1. |

### **Kenya – PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act, 2019)**

| Requirement | Details |
|----------|--------|
| **Territorial Scope** | Applies to any processing of personal data in Kenya, including data processed by foreign entities if the data is used or stored in Kenya. Payment flows involving Kenyan users are fully covered. |
| **Lawful Basis Options** | - Consent (opt-in required)<br>- Legitimate interest — *only where no alternative exists and minimal impact*<br>→ Fraud detection using behavioral analytics is **not valid** unless consented or justified by legitimate interest with safeguards. |
| **Cross-Border Transfers** | - Requires SCCs or other approved mechanisms<br>• SCCs are acceptable if approved by the NAPDA<br>• No adequacy decisions for Kenya<br>→ Must use SCCs with NAPDA-approved templates. |
| **Data Subject Rights** | - Right to access, erasure, rectification, and objection<br>- Response window: **30 calendar days** from request receipt<br>- Financial records may be retained longer (up to 10 years) — *no exception for deletion*<br>⚠️ Erasure of transactional data is not permitted if it affects financial obligations. |
| **Breach Notification** | - Must notify NAPDA within **48 hours** of discovery<br>- Public disclosure required only after 30 days (if material)<br>- No public notification for minor breaches. |
| **Registration/Representation** | - Mandatory appointment of a **local representative** in Kenya — *required under Section 12*<br>→ Must be appointed by week 6 of Phase 1. |

### **Poland – GDPR (with local enforcement via PDPB)**

| Requirement | Details |
|----------|--------|
| **Territorial Scope** | Applies to any processing of personal data in Poland, including data processed by foreign entities if the data is used or stored in Poland. Payment flows involving Polish users are fully covered. |
| **Lawful Basis Options** | - Consent (opt-in required)<br>- Legitimate interest — *only where no alternative exists and minimal impact*<br>→ Fraud detection using behavioral analytics is **not valid** unless consented or justified by legitimate interest with safeguards. |
| **Cross-Border Transfers** | - Requires SCCs or other approved mechanisms<br>• SCCs are acceptable if approved by the PDPB<br>• No adequacy decisions for Poland<br>→ Must use SCCs with PDPB-approved templates. |
| **Data Subject Rights** | - Right to access, erasure, rectification, and objection<br>- Response window: **30 calendar days** from request receipt<br>- Financial records may be retained longer (up to 15 years) — *no exception for deletion*<br>⚠️ Erasure of transactional data is not permitted if it affects financial obligations. |
| **Breach Notification** | - Must notify PDPB within **72 hours** of discovery<br>- Public disclosure required after 30 days (if material)<br>- No public notification for minor breaches. |
| **Registration/Representation** | - Mandatory appointment of a **local representative** in Poland — *required under Article 28*<br>→ Must be appointed by week 8 of Phase 1. |

### **Key Conflicts with Each Other and Group Program**

| Conflict | Description |
|--------|------------|
| **Brazil vs Kenya (Response Windows)** | Brazil requires 15-day response window; Kenya requires 30 days — *conflict in data subject rights handling*. This creates a **sequencing issue**: Brazil launch must have faster response capability than Kenya. |
| **Kenya vs Poland (Financial Record Retention)** | Kenya and Poland allow up to 10–15 years of financial record retention; group program assumes deletion after 7 years — *gap in records-of-processing inventory*. |
| **Group Program vs All Jurisdictions (Consent Architecture)** | Group’s cookie consent manager is designed for web properties only. Payment flows involve mobile and backend processing — *no support for device-level or transactional consent* → requires **forking** per market. |
| **Cross-Border Transfer Mechanism Inconsistencies** | All three require SCCs, but Brazil and Kenya have different approval timelines (ANPD vs NAPDA) — *creates a delay in cross-border transfer readiness*. |

> ✅ These conflicts drive the sequencing:  
> - **Brazil launches first**, due to shorter response window and earlier availability of SCC templates.  
> - **Kenya follows 90 days later** because it requires longer data subject response windows (30 days) — cannot be supported in parallel with Brazil’s 15-day requirement.  
> - **Poland last** due to long retention periods and PDPB approval timelines.

### **Key Sequencing Rationale**

- **Brazil first** because:  
  - Shorter data subject response window (15 days) — requires faster implementation than Kenya or Poland.  
  - Earlier availability of SCC templates from ANPD.  
- **Kenya after Brazil** due to longer response windows and different legal interpretation timelines.  
- **Poland last** due to long financial record retention periods and PDPB approval delays.

> ✅ All workstreams are sequenced to avoid parallel engineering work with platform migration (which occupies Q2–Q3).  
> ⚠️ No workstream can complete before its market launch — all carry a **compensating control**:

| Workstream | Compensating Control |
|----------|------------------------|
| W1.2, W2.1, W3.2 | In-place fallback consent model with opt-out capability for fraud detection use cases (pre-approved by DPO) |
| W1.3, W2.2, W3.3 | Manual escalation path to legal team for data subject requests during launch phase |

| R6 | Cross-border transfer mechanism not approved by local regulator (e.g., ANPD, NAPDA) | Medium | High | Legal Counsel (Brazil/Kenya/Poland) | Pre-approve SCC templates with regulators; conduct mock filings | No approval received within 14 days of submission |
| R7 | Engineering capacity constrained by platform migration (Q2–Q3) | High | Medium | Engineering Lead | Front-load legal and process workstreams before engineering implementation | Platform migration timeline slips by >5 business days |
| R8 | Inconsistent consent architecture across markets leads to user confusion | Medium | Medium | Product Owner | Implement unified UX with market-specific toggle for data use | User feedback indicates inconsistent consent messaging |
| R9 | Failure to appoint local representative in time (Brazil, Kenya, Poland) | High | High | Group DPO | Assign dedicated legal liaison per market; set appointment deadlines at week 4/6/8 | Appointment not filed by deadline |
| R10 | Data breach involving financial records leads to reputational damage | High | High | Security Operations + PR | Conduct post-breach communication plan with pre-approved messaging | Breach involves financial data and affects >5,000 users |
| R11 | Vendor due diligence fails to identify fraud detection use cases | Medium | High | Vendor Risk Manager | Require explicit disclosure of fraud detection logic in vendor questionnaires | Vendor response lacks detail on behavioral analytics |
| R12 | Group program assumes deletion after 7 years (violates Poland/Kenya) | High | High | Data Governance Officer | Build jurisdiction-specific retention schedules into records-of-processing | Retention period not updated in records-of-processing inventory |

> 🔍 **Likelihood & Impact Definitions**  
> - **Low**: Unlikely to occur; minimal impact if it does.  
> - **Medium**: Possible occurrence; moderate operational or financial impact.  
> - **High**: Likely to occur under normal conditions; significant risk of regulatory, reputational, or financial consequences.
