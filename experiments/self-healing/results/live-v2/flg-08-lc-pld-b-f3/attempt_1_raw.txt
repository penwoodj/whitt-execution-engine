3. Whether to implement mandatory data localization for transactional logs in Brazil and Kenya, or allow processing in the group’s primary region.

**Critical Path:** Legal & process workstreams → Consent architecture design → Engineering implementation → Market-specific data flows → Launch readiness.  
*The critical path spans from Week 10 to Week 48 and is constrained by engineering capacity shared with platform migration.*

**Single Largest Risk (High impact, High likelihood):**  
> **Local regulator changes guidance mid-program**, particularly in Brazil under LGPD, which could invalidate existing data processing assumptions or require retroactive compliance actions. This risk is amplified if the group fails to maintain real-time regulatory monitoring.

## 2. Jurisdiction-by-Jurisdiction Requirements Analysis

### **Brazil (LGPD – Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados)**  
**Territorial Scope**: Applies to any processing of personal data in Brazil, including data collected from users or processed via third parties. *Applies if the firm processes data of Brazilian residents*, regardless of where the processing occurs.

**Lawful Basis for Fraud Prevention & Payment Processing**:  
- **Consent** (most common) – requires explicit, informed, and revocable consent.  
- **Legitimate Interest** – only permissible with strict safeguards; not allowed for fraud detection if it involves profiling or automated decision-making.  
- *No "legitimate interest" basis is permitted under LGPD for financial services due to risk of bias and lack of transparency.*

**Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms**:  
- Only via:  
  - Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) – approved by ANPD  
  - Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs) – requires group-level approval  
  - **Data localization required for transactional logs** in Brazil. Logs must be stored on servers within Brazil.  
- *No SCCs accepted without ANPD pre-approval.*

**Data Subject Rights**:  
- Right to access, rectification, erasure, and data portability  
- Response window: **10 business days** from request  
- **Exception for financial records**: Financial records (e.g., transaction logs) may be retained longer than 3 years; subject to audit by ANPD.  

**Breach Notification**:  
- Must notify DPA within **72 hours** of discovery  
- Public disclosure only if the breach poses a risk to individuals’ rights and is not trivial  

**Registration/Representation Obligations**:  
- Appointment of **local representative** required for any processing involving Brazilian residents  
- Registration with ANPD required at launch (30 days pre-launch)  

> 🔴 **Conflict with Group Program**:  
> - Current group consent manager does *not* support revocable, granular consent for financial services.  
> - Group records-of-processing inventory lacks field-level data categorization needed to meet LGPD’s transparency requirements.

---

### **Kenya (PDPA – Personal Data Protection Act 2019)**  
**Territorial Scope**: Applies to any processing of personal data in Kenya, including data collected from Kenyan residents or processed via third parties. *Applies if the firm processes data of Kenyan users.*

**Lawful Basis for Fraud Prevention & Payment Processing**:  
- **Consent** – required and must be specific, informed, and revocable  
- **Legitimate Interest** – permitted only with a clear justification and impact assessment; not allowed in fraud detection due to risk of profiling  

**Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms**:  
- SCCs accepted if approved by the Data Protection Commissioner (DPC)  
- No data localization requirement for transactional logs  
- *Group’s primary region may host logs, provided SCCs are valid and DPC-approved*  

**Data Subject Rights**:  
- Right to access, rectification, erasure, portability  
- Response window: **15 business days** from request  
- *No exception for financial records; all data must be accessible within the window*

**Breach Notification**:  
- Notify DPC within **30 days** of discovery  
- Public disclosure only if breach poses significant risk to individuals  

**Registration/Representation Obligations**:  
- Appointment of local representative required for any processing involving Kenyan residents  
- Registration with DPC required 60 days before launch  

> 🔴 **Conflict with Group Program**:  
> - Current consent manager is not designed for financial services or dynamic fraud rules.  
> - No mechanism to handle rights requests in real time; current runbook lacks automation.

- No data localization requirement for transactional logs  
- *Group’s primary region may host logs, provided SCCs are valid*  

**Data Subject Rights**:  
- Right to access, rectification, erasure, portability  
- Response window: **15 business days** from request  
- *No exception for financial records; all data must be accessible within the window*

**Breach Notification**:  
- Notify PDPB within **72 hours** of discovery  
- Public disclosure only if breach poses significant risk  

**Registration/Representation Obligations**:  
- Appointment of local representative required for any processing involving Polish residents  
- Registration with PDPB required 60 days before launch  

> 🔴 **Conflict with Group Program**:

### **Cross-Jurisdictional Conflicts Summary**

| Conflict | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Data Localization** | Brazil requires transaction logs stored locally; Kenya and Poland do not. This creates a conflict in data flow design. |
| **Consent Architecture** | LGPD (Brazil) requires granular, revocable consent; PDPA/PLD allow broader consent but require clarity. Group’s current consent manager is insufficient for financial services. |
| **Response Windows** | Kenya and Poland have 15-day windows; Brazil has 10-day window — creates inconsistency in rights handling timelines. |
| **Breach Notification Timelines** | Brazil (72h), Kenya (30d), Poland (72h) → inconsistent timing, requiring a unified breach response framework. |
| **Local Representation** | All three require local representatives; group currently lacks a regional representative model. |

> ⚠️ These conflicts drive the sequencing:  
> - **Brazil first**, due to strict data localization and early regulatory enforcement.  
> - **Kenya next**, because it shares similar consent requirements with Poland but has longer response windows, allowing more time for process design.  
> - **Poland last**, as it is less restrictive on data flows and allows shared architecture, but requires final validation of SCCs.
