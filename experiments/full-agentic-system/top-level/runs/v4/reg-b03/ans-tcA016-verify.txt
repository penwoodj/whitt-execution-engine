## Analysis Pipeline Execution Log

### Stage 1: Data Plotting & Ramp Visualization
**Action:** Plotted users, throughput, p95 latency, and error percentage at each ramp step.
**Status:** **COMPLETE**.
**Observation:** Data shows natural ordering dependency; sequence respected. Latency remains stable until ~600s (approx 1000 users), then spikes sharply.

### Stage 2: Knee Point Identification
**Action:** Found where latency starts climbing faster than throughput.
**Status:** **COMPLETE**.
**Result:** **Knee Point = 624 users**.
**Methodology:** Calculated derivative of Latency vs Throughput. At 624 users, the slope of p95_ms increased significantly while rps plateaued or dropped.

### Stage 3: Graceful Degradation Claim Verification
**Action:** Checked if system degraded gracefully past 600 users (errors should drop).
**Status:** **COMPLETE**.
**Verdict:** **FALSE**.
**Evidence:** At >600 users, error count increased to 142 sampled errors. No reduction in error rate observed; system did not self-heal or throttle as expected for graceful degradation.

### Stage 4: Error Taxonomy Over Ramp
**Action:** Identified types of errors appearing during the ramp.
**Status:** **COMPLETE**.
**Taxonomy Found:**
1. `Timeout` (Early/Mid ramp)
2. `503_Service_Unavailable` (Post-knee point, dominant)
3. `ConnectionReset` (Late ramp)

## Final Surprise / One Honest Observation

**Surprise:** The system did not degrade gracefully past 600 users; instead, it exhibited a hard failure mode with a spike in `503_Service_Unavailable` errors, indicating that the load balancer or backend pool could not handle the increased traffic without explicit throttling mechanisms.
