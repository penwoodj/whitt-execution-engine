### 2. Knee Detection Methodology

**Method:** Slope Analysis
**Threshold Ratio:** 2.5x increase in latency relative to throughput drop or absolute latency jump.

**Observation:**
At **User Count = 620**, the system exhibited a sudden change in behavior:
- Latency jumped from **45 ms** (stable baseline) to **180 ms**.
- Throughput dropped from **950 rps** to **720 rps**.

This indicates that the "knee" of the performance curve was reached at 620 users, where the system could no longer maintain stable latency or throughput.

### 3. Graceful Degradation Claim Verdict

**Claim:** The system was supposed to degrade gracefully past 600 users.
**Verdict:** **FAILED**

**Evidence:**
- At 600 users (approximate threshold), the system should have shown a gradual increase in latency and decrease in throughput.
- Instead, at exactly **620 users**, there was an abrupt spike:
  - Latency increased by **135 ms** (from 45ms to 180ms).
  - Throughput decreased by **230 rps** (from 950rps to 720rps).

This behavior suggests a hard failure or resource exhaustion rather than graceful degradation. The system did not handle the load increase smoothly past the 600-user mark.

| **Throughput Drop** | 620 | High | Significant reduction in requests processed per second, correlating with latency spike. |

### Rules Documented (`rules.md`)

- **Knee Detection Threshold:** Latency must increase by >2.5x or absolute jump >40ms relative to baseline.
  - *Reason:* Prevents false positives from minor fluctuations; ensures significant stress events are captured.
- **Graceful Degradation Check:** System must show gradual latency increase and throughput decrease without abrupt spikes.
  - *Reason:* Validates system resilience under load; abrupt spikes indicate failure modes.
