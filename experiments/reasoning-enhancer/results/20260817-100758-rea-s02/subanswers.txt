1. A canary deploy is a deployment strategy where a small percentage of users are exposed to a new version of a software application before the full rollout.

2. The key components of a canary deploy include traffic splitting, monitoring, rollback capability, and gradual user exposure.

3. The purpose of a canary deploy is to minimize risk by testing a new software version with a small subset of users before expanding it to all users.

4. The process for deploying a canary involves selecting a small group of users, routing their traffic to the new version, monitoring performance and errors, and gradually increasing exposure if no issues are detected.

5. Constraints or requirements for a canary deploy include real-time monitoring, defined thresholds for failure detection, rollback mechanisms, and precise control over user segmentation.
